The Last Greenhouse

At the edge of the city, a glass greenhouse survived between two new apartment blocks. Every morning, Inez unlocked its door before sunrise and watered the tomato plants her grandmother had left behind.

One Monday, she found a paper crane on the workbench. Its wings were folded from a page of the neighborhood's old map. On the back, someone had written a street name that no longer existed.

Inez followed the faded route past the tram depot and down to the river. Beneath the third stone bridge, a small iron box waited in the mud. It held a packet of seeds and a note: Plant these where the city forgot to look.

By spring, green shoots had appeared in the empty lot beside the greenhouse. Neighbors began bringing pots, then chairs, then stories. The lot became a garden, and the garden became a place to stay awhile.
